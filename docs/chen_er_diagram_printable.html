2. Examiner Viva Defense Q&A on Chen Notation

Question 1: Explain how you differentiated between strong and weak entities in Chen notation.
Answer: Strong entities (e.g., USER, PRODUCT, DROP, ORDER) are drawn with single rectangles and possess full primary keys that uniquely identify each instance. Weak entities (ORDER_ITEM, WALLET_LEDGER) are drawn with double rectangles; they lack a primary key on their own and only possess a partial key (discriminator, drawn with dashed underline). They connect to their identifying owner entities via double diamonds with double lines representing total existence dependency.

Question 2: Where did you use composite, multivalued, and derived attributes?
Answer: In our Chen model: 1. Composite Attribute: Name branches into sub-ovals first_name and last_name. 2. Multivalued Attribute: attributes in PRODUCT is drawn with a double oval because a product contains multiple specifications (size, color, edition). 3. Derived Attribute: available_qty in INVENTORY is drawn with a dashed oval because it is dynamically computed using the equation: total_allocated - sold_quantity - reserved_holds; balance in WALLET is computed from ledger credits minus debits.

Question 3: How does Crow's-foot notation differ from Peter Chen notation?
Answer: Peter Chen notation (the academic textbook standard) explicitly separates entities (rectangles), relationships (diamonds), and attributes (ovals), displaying cardinality ratios (1, N, M) and participation (single vs double lines). Crow's-foot notation is a physical database modeling convention where attributes are placed directly inside rectangular table boxes and relationship cardinalities are shown via line-ending symbols (e.g. || for one, |< for many).
